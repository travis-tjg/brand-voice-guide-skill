# Client sign-off sheet

This is the only thing we ask the client to do. It goes with page 1 of the guide, in the email body or as a one-page attachment, and never inside the guide itself. You prepare it; the user sends it. Never email the client yourself, even if you have the tools. Once the answers are logged in the ledger, archive it.

## Rules

- **Draft, don't ask.** Every item shows what we found, with the source, and our drafted answer in the exact words we'd publish. The client ticks it or corrects one line.
- **Only facts that block copy get an item.** Preferences get a sensible default, which the client can change at sign-off.
- **Up to 8 items, each up to 60 words including the tick boxes; aim for 5 or fewer.** The checker counts them (`--sheet`). Put the items that unlock the most copy first, and turn the rest into defaults.
- **Never ask what research already answered.** Never ask the client to describe their voice, their personality or their competitors. Those are our job.
- **In an industry we don't know well, include a customer item.** It's our draft of who their customer is, for the client to confirm or correct, so they get the final say without writing it themselves.
- **Dates are one item each.** When a source gives a count ("over 12 years") and no date, draft the date-anchored version and ask for the year.
- **Silence approves the voice and structure, never the facts.** Unanswered items stay needed and out of copy. So does any answer like "maybe" or "I think so". Page 1 shows a sample line (1.3), so the client has seen the voice they're approving.
- **Log every answer.** Write it into the ledger and the dates table with the date and how it arrived, for example "client, email 2026-11-03". Update the fact's wording if the client corrected it, then regenerate the proof list.
- **If there's no reply by the date,** the user books a 15-minute call, goes through page 1 and this sheet on screen, and records it with the client's OK. The recording is the source for each answer.
- **If the client objects to the voice,** ask which line felt wrong and change the rule behind it, then log it as a ruling in section 9.

## Template

```text
Subject: [Business name] brand guide: [n] facts to check

Hi [first name],

We've drafted your brand guide; page 1 is attached. It's what we'll use for everything we write for you, including how we'll sound.

Below are [n] facts we couldn't confirm from public sources. Each one has our best draft. Reply "all correct", or fix any line.

If we don't hear back by [date], we'll start writing with the voice as drafted and leave these facts out until you confirm them.

1. [Label]
   We found: [what, and where]
   We'd write: "[exact wording]"
   [ ] Correct   [ ] Change to: ____________
   Needed for: [where it will appear]

2. ...
```

## Example (illustrative)

An invented electrician in [City]. The business, the license number and the facts are all made up.

```text
1. Your license
   We found: master electrician license E-00000 on the [State] board's lookup, with no issue date. Your About page says "licensed for over 12 years".
   We'd write: "Licensed master electrician in [State] since 2014"
   [ ] Correct   [ ] The year is: ______
   Needed for: every service page, your Google Business Profile description, all directory profiles

2. EV chargers
   We found: your site lists Level 2 home EV charger installation. We found no manufacturer certification.
   We'd write: "We install Level 2 home EV chargers."
   [ ] Correct   [ ] We're certified by: ____________
   Needed for: the EV charger page and your Google services list

3. Who does the work
   We found: reviews mention two electricians by name. Nothing says whether you use subcontractors.
   We'd write: "Every job is done by a licensed electrician on our own team."
   [ ] Correct   [ ] Change to: ____________
   Needed for: the home page and every service page

4. Same-day service
   We found: your site says "same-day service available". Your Google hours are Monday to Friday, 7 to 5.
   We'd write: "Same-day service Monday to Friday."
   [ ] Correct   [ ] Change to: ____________
   Needed for: the emergency page and your Google description

5. Your customer (our draft)
   We'd write: "Homeowners in [City], often in older houses, calling because something stopped working or they're adding something big, like an EV charger or a generator."
   [ ] Correct   [ ] Change to: ____________
   Needed for: the voice of everything we write
```

Why this example works:
- **Item 1** turns a year count into a date anchor, which doesn't go stale. The client checks one number instead of writing a sentence.
- **Item 2** writes what the client does, not a certification we couldn't find.
- **Item 3** comes from a question shoppers ask: who actually does the work.
- **Item 4** settles a conflict between two of the client's own sources instead of picking one, and the draft uses only what both sources support.
- **Item 5** gives the client the final say on their customer without making them write it.
