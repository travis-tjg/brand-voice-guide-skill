# Examples: generic lines and proof-specific lines

**Everything here is invented.** The businesses, people, license numbers, years and courses were made up to show the method. None of them is a real business or a client.

**The test:** could this sentence appear unchanged on any site in this category, in any city? If it could, it's generic. A good line names something a stranger could check, and every fact in it traces to a ledger row. The IDs in brackets show which row; real copy doesn't show them.

Writers copy examples. So every good line below uses only public or confirmed facts, most of them start with the reader rather than the business, and so must the sample lines in a real guide. The openers start in different ways on purpose: if every example opened with a question, models would learn that.

## 1. A DUI defense attorney in [City] (illustrative)

Invented ledger:
- **F01:** Assistant district attorney, [County], 2004 to 2010 (public)
- **F02:** Licensed in [State] since 2003 (public)
- **F03:** Completed a standardized field sobriety testing course on how the tests are given and scored, 2016 (public, on the trainer's site)
- **F04:** Defends DUI cases in [City], handling both the criminal case and the DMV license hearing (public, site)
- **F05:** Solo practice; she appears at every court date and DMV hearing herself (confirmed)
- **F06:** Free phone consultation (public)

**Home page, opening line**
- Poor: "Our experienced DUI attorneys provide aggressive, personalized defense to protect your rights and your future."
- Good: "Charged with DUI in [City]? [Name] handles both the criminal case and the DMV license hearing. She prosecuted cases for [County] from 2004 to 2010." [F01, F04]
- Why: "Experienced", "aggressive" and "protect your rights" fit almost any DUI site. The good line starts with the reader's situation, then gives a role, a place and years a stranger could look up.

**Service page**
- Poor: "We have extensive knowledge of the science behind field sobriety and breath tests."
- Good: "If an officer had you walk a line or follow a pen with your eyes, those were field sobriety tests. In 2016, [Name] completed a course on how those tests are given and scored." [F03]
- Why: The good line starts from what happened to the reader, then gives the proof with a year. It says what the course covered and stops there; it doesn't claim expertise the ledger can't back.

**Shopper question** (in our test run, the engine told shoppers to ask "Will you personally handle my case?")
- Poor: "Our team of skilled professionals will handle every aspect of your case."
- Good: "Yes. [Name] handles your court dates and your DMV hearing herself. There's no associate to hand you off to." [F04, F05]
- Why: Engines tell shoppers to ask this, so the site should answer it plainly. F05 is confirmed, so the writer can say it.

**Upgrade trap**
- Poor: "Certified field sobriety testing expert with 20+ years of experience."
- Good: "Licensed since 2003. Completed field sobriety testing training, 2016." [F02, F03]
- Why: Training isn't certification, and "expert" goes beyond what F03 says. "20+ years" goes stale and drifts across profiles; a date anchor doesn't.

## 2. A plumbing company in [City] (illustrative)

Invented ledger:
- **F01:** Owner [Name] has held [State] master plumber license M-00000 since 2011 (public, state lookup)
- **F02:** In business in [City] since 2013 (public, state business record)
- **F03:** Same-day service Monday to Saturday, 7am to 7pm (public, GBP hours and site)
- **F04:** Price agreed in writing before work starts (public, site)
- **F05:** Two-year warranty on labor (public, site)

**Home page, opening line**
- Poor: "Your trusted local plumbing experts, delivering quality service you can count on, 24/7."
- Good: "Call Monday to Saturday, 7am to 7pm, for same-day service, and you'll agree the price in writing before any work starts. Our owner, [Name], is a licensed master plumber." [F01, F03, F04]
- Why: "Trusted", "quality" and "experts" can't be checked, and "24/7" isn't true for this company. The good line says what they do offer, so it never has to mention what they don't. It also keeps "master plumber" singular, because the ledger only shows the owner's license.

**About page**
- Poor: "With decades of combined experience, our team is passionate about plumbing."
- Good: "[Name] has held a [State] master plumber license since 2011. The company has been in business in [City] since 2013." [F01, F02]
- Why: "Decades of combined experience" is what writers reach for when they don't have a confirmed fact. Use the fact, or leave it out.

**Shopper question** (illustrative: say the engines asked shoppers "Is water leaking right now?")
- Poor: "We offer fast emergency plumbing whenever you need us."
- Good: "Water leaking now? Turn off the main shutoff valve, then call us. We do same-day visits Monday to Saturday, 7am to 7pm." [F03]
- Why: The good line answers the question the shopper actually has, and it states the real hours.

**Google Business Profile description** (who, what, where)
- Poor: "A leading plumbing company dedicated to excellence and customer satisfaction."
- Good: "[Company] is a plumbing company in [City], [State], run by master plumber [Name] (license M-00000). Same-day service Monday to Saturday. Prices agreed before work starts. Two-year labor warranty." [F01, F03, F04, F05]
- Why: It's the same proof points the site uses, in the same words, so the shopper and the engines see one consistent picture. A description is the one place the business goes first, because it's answering "who is this?"

## 3. A med spa in [City] (illustrative)

Med spas are a regulated field. Treat every claim about results, credentials or comparisons as one the client must approve, and treat no rule as known until the client or their advisor checks it.

Invented ledger:
- **F01:** Medical director [Name], MD, board certified in dermatology by the American Board of Dermatology (public, the board's lookup)
- **F02:** Every injection is given by a registered nurse (confirmed)
- **F03:** Each nurse injector has completed [manufacturer]'s [course name] (confirmed, with course certificates)
- **F04:** Free 30-minute consultation in person (public, site)
- **F05:** The medical director reviews every new patient's treatment plan (confirmed)
- **F06:** Shows before-and-after photos of its own patients, shared with their consent, at consultations (confirmed)

**Home page, opening line**
- Poor: "Look and feel your best with our luxurious, results-driven aesthetic treatments."
- Good: "If you're thinking about injections, start with who gives them. Here, it's always a registered nurse, and our medical director, [Name], MD, is board certified in dermatology. Your first visit can be a free 30-minute consultation." [F01, F02, F04]
- Why: "Luxurious" and "results-driven" fit every med spa. Who holds the needle is something shoppers ask about, and the good line answers it. It says "medical director", not "under the supervision of", because the ledger doesn't say how supervision works.

**Results claim**
- Poor: "Guaranteed natural-looking results that take years off your face."
- Good: "At your free consultation, we'll show you before-and-after photos of our own patients who had the treatment you're considering." [F04, F06]
- Why: A guarantee is a claim the client must approve, and most can't stand behind it. The good line offers evidence instead of a promise, and the offer itself is on the ledger.

**Upgrade trap**
- Poor: "Our expert injectors are [manufacturer]-certified."
- Good: "Each of our nurse injectors has completed [manufacturer]'s [course name]." [F03]
- Why: Finishing a course is a certification only if the manufacturer calls it one. Use the course name exactly as the manufacturer lists it.

**Shopper question** ("Who will do my injections?")
- Poor: "Our highly trained team of professionals will take great care of you."
- Good: "A registered nurse gives every injection. Dr. [Name], our medical director, reviews every new patient's treatment plan." [F02, F05]
- Why: It names who does the work and who checks it. Both facts were confirmed by the client.

## Patterns across all three

| Generic | Proof-specific |
|---|---|
| experienced, seasoned | "licensed since [year]"; "former [role], [years]" |
| expert, specialist | the training or credential, named the way the issuer names it |
| trusted, top-rated, best | the rating with its count and month; an award with its year |
| quality service | what actually happens: the price agreed first, the real hours, who does the work |
| 24/7, free [X] (when not offered) | the real hours and offers from the ledger |
| decades of combined experience, over [n] years | the confirmed date, or nothing |

## When an SEO term is also a claim

Say the SEO brief targets "best plumber [City]". The term goes in, but never as a claim about the client.

- Poor: "We're the best plumber in [City]."
- Good, as an FAQ in a term-filling section: "**How do I choose the best plumber in [City]?** Start with the license. In [State], you can look up any plumber on the [board] site. Ours is M-00000." [F01]
- Why: The term appears as the shopper's own question. The answer pairs a selection criterion (licensing) with the client's proof, so the section serves the term, the shopper and the voice. The checker warns on it rather than failing, because "best" sits inside a question.

## Same facts, two voices

Facts make copy accurate. The voice makes it sound like one company and not the next. Two invented plumbing companies with the same F03 and F04, written for different customers:

- We sound like "a neighbor who happens to be a master plumber": "Water where it shouldn't be? Call us. We can come out the same day, Monday to Saturday, and we'll agree the price with you in writing before we start." [F03, F04]
- We sound like "the contractor a property manager can hand a work order to": "Same-day service, Monday to Saturday, 7am to 7pm. Every job starts with a written price you approve." [F03, F04]
- Why: both are accurate. The role, chosen for each company's customer, sets the rhythm, the warmth and who the sentence is about. Short and plain is a fine voice when it's chosen for the reader; it's a problem when it's the default. A guide that lists only facts leaves the voice to the model, and the model's default is the category's voice.

## Patterns that read as AI-written

Whatever the voice, cut these before delivering. Banning a word only pushes a model to its synonym, so these are structures as well as words.

- Openers like "Whether you're a... or a..." and "When it comes to..."
- "Not just X, but Y"
- Three adjectives in a row
- A question opening every section
- A closing line that sums up what was just said
- Stock phrases such as "peace of mind", "navigate", "tailored", "seamless", "rest assured" and "don't hesitate to"
- Empathy lines like "we understand how stressful this can be". Show empathy with a detail from reviews instead.
